# Paradox Doctor user-demand research (2026-09-07)

This internal product note summarizes public modding pain points observed in Reddit, Paradox forums, and existing validator/tool ecosystems before the HOI4 + Victoria 3 expansion.

## HOI4 recurring pain points
- Unexpected token / brace-balance failures can make only part of a focus tree load.
- Localization format, duplicate keys, missing keys, and encoding issues are frequent.
- Unknown/invalid effects and wrong scope cause difficult-to-interpret errors.
- Map/state editing commonly fails because of missing buildings, overlapping/duplicate province assignments, strategic-region mistakes, or stale nudge-tool output.
- error.log is noisy; modders often need help separating likely root causes from cascaded errors.

## Victoria 3 recurring pain points
- Localization filename/path/encoding/header formatting is a common beginner failure.
- Jomini scope errors (unset/wrong scope, event-target links) are hard to interpret.
- Journal entries/events have sparse learning material, and generic LLMs often invent invalid syntax.
- State/map changes can crash with poor diagnostics.
- Mod conflicts/load-order issues are difficult to isolate, while crash logs may be less useful than expected.
- File-path conventions and history-file naming can silently prevent changes from loading.

## Competitive landscape
- CWTools and Tiger offer strong validator functionality, but are primarily installation/developer workflows; per-game validation depth varies, especially for Victoria 3.
- Existing visual modding tools focus more on content creation than browser-based diagnosis.

## Product direction
Paradox Doctor should differentiate as a zero-install, local-first diagnostic product: choose a game, scan folder + error.log together, prioritize likely root causes, explain errors in plain language, provide game-specific checks, export a shareable report, and link users to focused troubleshooting guides.
